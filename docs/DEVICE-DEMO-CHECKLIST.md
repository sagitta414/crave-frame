# Crave Frame: three-minute device demonstration

Use the local build containing the new branding. These are manual acceptance steps, not completed test results.

## Before recording
- Open the TV view on the actual Fire TV and connect a phone to the same reachable environment. A phone cannot reach the computer using 127.0.0.1.
- Pick an eligible evening with a reviewed meal photo and a specific pairing explanation. Keep real dietary and viewing preferences active.
- Confirm the logo and dinner name are readable from the couch. Check both 720p and 1080p if available.

## Demonstration
1. **0:00–0:20 — The problem:** “We spend too long deciding what to eat and watch. Crave Frame turns those two decisions into one evening.”
2. **0:20–0:55 — Choose:** Use only the remote to inspect the featured dinner and title, explain whether the connection is story-inspired or mood-based, and select Plan this evening.
3. **0:55–1:25 — Handoff:** Scan the cooking QR code. Show the same evening on the phone and TV.
4. **1:25–2:10 — Cook:** Complete one step on the phone. Show the TV catching up. Apply a 15-minute delay and compare the dashboard and live timeline.
5. **2:10–2:40 — Recover:** Disconnect and reconnect the phone. Verify the completed step remains completed and the delay was applied only once.
6. **2:40–3:00 — Close:** “Less deciding. More time together.” Optional memories can be mentioned after the core flow, without requiring viewers to complete one.

## Pass/fail record
Record device model, viewport, build/release, and observed result for each:
- Remote reaches every visible action; focus is visible.
- Back closes details before leaving the page.
- Down and Up scroll without trapping focus.
- Phone and TV show identical dinner, recipe adaptation, progress, and schedule.
- Reconnection retains progress without duplicate actions.
- Logo silhouette, title, and primary button are legible from the couch.
- Narrow phone layout has no clipped navigation or hidden actions.

Do not mark physical-device checks passed based on automated tests alone.
